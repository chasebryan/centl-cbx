# CBX mathematical design language

**Status:** active visual/research design contract for `cbx.kernel`  
**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Scope:** mathematical diagrams, state-machine visuals, recursive telemetry, research notes, and public CBX graphics

CBX uses three related directional objects:

1. the **Bryan Entanglement Cross** (`BEC`), the primitive four-direction grammar;
2. the **Bryan Entanglement Compass** (`BEC-C`), the eight-direction finite composite projection;
3. the **Bryan Recursive Entanglement Calculus** (`BREC`), the recursively closed history language behind both projections.

The canonical BREC v1.0 specification is maintained in the CENTL research corpus. The exact CBX/Erdős–Straus application contract is local:

- [`BREC-ES-APPLICATION.md`](BREC-ES-APPLICATION.md)

The central hierarchy is therefore:

```text
BREC recursive history space
    -> Bryan Entanglement Cross      primitive projection
    -> Bryan Entanglement Compass    finite composite projection
    -> CBX exact application         arithmetic-labelled observed histories
```

The Cross and Compass are not competitors with BREC. They are readable coordinate charts over a recursively larger object.

---

## 1. Identity split

The FCF camel and FCF wordmark remain organizational identity.

The Cross, Compass, and BREC notation are **CBX mathematical emblems/language**.

Use them to communicate how CBX interprets exact state pressure and transition structure. They should not replace the FCF identity where the subject is the Foundation rather than the kernel mathematics.

---

## 2. Cross usage

Use the Cross for primitive transition narratives:

```text
L = ←⊖
R = →⊕
U = ↑(⊕/⊖)
D = ↓(⊖/⊕)
```

In binary BREC notation this is the finite projection

```text
R = +
L = -
U = +-
D = -+
```

Examples:

- exact miss versus exact construction;
- live BEC paths such as `L^jR`;
- expansion of the mathematical state space;
- excavation into a smaller exact residual problem.

The Cross is the preferred symbol when four directions are enough.

---

## 3. Compass usage

Use the Compass when a state or research operation has a genuine composite directional structure.

The four diagonal schemes are fixed:

```text
UL = + / − / −
UR = + / + / −
DL = − / − / +
DR = − / + / +
```

They are derived from the Cross, not assigned aesthetically.

```text
U = (+,−)      D = (−,+)
L = −          R = +

(V1,V2) ⊗ H = (V1,H,V2)
```

Therefore:

```text
U⊗L = +−−
U⊗R = ++−
D⊗L = −−+
D⊗R = −++
```

The Compass is the preferred symbol for second-order CBX state maps, scheduler visualizations, composite transition diagrams, and kernel artwork intended to suggest navigation through exact arithmetic pressure.

The Compass does **not** exhaust the third BREC layer. The additional depth-3 histories

```text
+++
+-+
-+-
---
```

remain legitimate recursive states. At depth `n`, BREC has `2^n` formal binary histories.

---

## 4. BREC usage

Use BREC whenever CBX must retain more than one finite direction label or must reason about a sequence of exact constructive/obstructive outcomes.

Canonical recursion:

```text
E_empty = E
E_(w sigma) = T_sigma(E_w),    sigma in {+,-}
```

Canonical finite closure through depth `N` contains

```text
2^(N+1) - 1
```

formal histories including the empty history.

BREC distinguishes **formal history identity** from **evaluated arithmetic state identity**. Two different histories may eventually describe the same domain state; that is a collision to study, not permission to erase provenance.

For CBX Lane I, `cbx-brec-i` applies the exact map

```text
+  <=>  delta_k((p+k)/4) = 0
-  <=>  exact defined miss
?  <=>  undefined application stage
```

and streams every observed contiguous motif through a selected recursion order. Default order 4 is intentionally larger than the eight-ray Compass projection.

## 5. Visual contract

For formal CBX diagrams:

- keep the center as `ε` or a clearly identified exact state object;
- use four equal cardinal rays for the Cross;
- use eight equal rays for the Compass;
- preserve all cardinal and diagonal labels exactly;
- when drawing a hypercube, distinguish formal vertices from evaluated-state collisions;
- prefer black/white or one-color high-contrast rendering;
- keep typography mathematical and sparse;
- avoid extra decoration inside the state diagram;
- do not add arrows or sign sequences whose semantics are not defined in the corresponding research contract.

For public artwork, visual stylization is allowed around the diagram, but the mathematical core should remain legible and unchanged.

---

## 6. Mathematical safety contract

None of these design objects changes CBX verdict semantics.

```text
W -> I -> N -> L
```

remains the production-equivalent verdict order.

The Cross, Compass, and BREC telemetry are downstream of exact arithmetic:

```text
exact arithmetic
    -> exact ancestry / exact predicate result
        -> proved finite state effect
            -> BEC / BEC-C / BREC annotation
                -> telemetry / visualization / scheduler research
```

No graphical direction, recursive word, motif frequency, absent finite motif, finite target coincidence, or finite cylinder collapse grants pruning permission or establishes a theorem.

An optimization that uses BREC evidence must still preserve exact search semantics independently.

A proposed arithmetic pruning rule derived from BREC must pass the stricter route:

```text
finite observation
    -> explicit candidate statement
        -> adversarial larger-scale search
            -> preserve every falsifier
                -> exact proof
                    -> independent verifier
                        -> pruning authority
```

A finite candidate that fails later is not to be hidden or silently removed. Its falsifier becomes part of the regression corpus. The `k=23` ancestry-coincidence episode is the canonical example: the two-million-prime pattern was real, but larger exact primes falsified its universal extrapolation.

## 7. Naming contract

Use these exact names in repository and public research language:

```text
Bryan Entanglement Cross                BEC
Bryan Entanglement Compass              BEC-C
Bryan Recursive Entanglement Calculus   BREC
```

Recommended wording:

> The Bryan Entanglement Cross is the primitive directional grammar. The Bryan Entanglement Compass is its eight-direction finite composite projection. The Bryan Recursive Entanglement Calculus is the recursively closed history space behind them, and CBX attaches those histories only after exact arithmetic has determined the state effects.

Do not call the Compass a replacement, revision, or correction of the Cross. Do not describe BREC as an Erdős–Straus proof mechanism by itself.

---

## 8. CBX design interpretation

CBX is an X-ray kernel. Its job is not merely to emit a binary verdict, but to expose hidden exact structure that a short-circuit production stack would otherwise mask.

The Cross expresses primitive direction.

The Compass expresses selected finite compositions.

BREC preserves the recursive ancestry beyond those eight visible rays.

That distinction matters computationally: a prime can survive several obstructive exact states, reverse into a construction, re-enter obstruction, and later construct again. Flattening such a path to one label destroys information. BREC lets CBX retain the path while keeping the arithmetic itself sovereign.

The same principle applies to research failure. A false finite pattern can itself have a recursive history: observed contraction, later re-entry, explicit falsifier. CBX preserves that history too, so the machine learns where a tempting simplification stops being exact without ever converting the failed heuristic into proof authority.
